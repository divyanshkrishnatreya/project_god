# Panel Rules - Leela.exe

## Purpose
These rules control pacing, readability, and cinematic continuity across pages.

## Reading Flow
1. Panels must support a clear reading rhythm.
2. Eye flow should be guided by gaze, wave direction, lighting contrast, and panel shape.
3. Avoid layouts that confuse which panel lands as the emotional final beat.

## Default Panel Density
| Scene Function | Typical Panel Count |
| --- | --- |
| Atmospheric setup | 3 to 5 panels |
| Dialogue and reaction | 4 to 6 panels |
| Revelation beat | 1 to 4 panels |
| Exposition montage | 3 to 6 panels |
| Action escalation | 3 to 6 panels |

Use fewer, larger panels when awe or dread needs space.

## Established Page 1 Layout Signal
Page 1 uses a hybrid structure:
- title integrated into the first panel block
- domestic dialogue panels
- one major catastrophe panel
- mythic recap panels at the bottom

This means chapter pages are allowed to shift modes within a single page if the transitions remain readable and emotionally purposeful.

## Shot Progression
Within a page, prefer a readable flow such as:
1. establish place or subject
2. narrow into character focus
3. land on the emotional or revelatory beat

Not every page needs all three, but most should feel intentionally staged.

## Panel Types
### Establishing Panels
- orient geography
- show weather, time of day, water condition, and spatial relationships

### Reaction Panels
- prioritize eyes, breath, hand movement, and posture shifts
- allow space for silence

### Revelation Panels
- simplify background clutter
- use contrast and composition to isolate the new truth

### Montage Panels
- use for mythic exposition, flashback compression, or parallel events
- maintain a consistent narration voice if caption boxes are used

## Splash And Half-Splash Usage
- reserve for major reveals, divine scale, or chapter turns
- if using a splash, make sure the page before it earns the expansion
- do not use large format panels so often that scale loses meaning

## Dialogue Placement Guidance
Even when generating image prompts without final lettering:
1. leave breathable negative space in likely balloon zones
2. avoid putting vital facial acting where text will probably sit
3. keep important silhouettes from being blocked by future captions

## Continuity Between Panels
Track these from one panel to the next:
- character position
- facing direction
- prop handoff
- wetness, damage, dirt, and fatigue
- time of day and weather progression
- water level and wave behavior
- distance between characters

## Emotional Pacing
1. Let tonal changes earn themselves.
2. If a page moves from playful to catastrophic, use one hinge panel or beat to make the transition legible.
3. Matsya's scale changes should create escalation beats.
4. Use silence around water, sky, and revelation whenever possible.

## Ten-Page Chapter Rule
Because each avatar chapter is only 10 pages long:
1. Every page must carry unique story progress.
2. Page 7 or earlier should make the chapter's endgame visible.
3. Page 10 should resolve the avatar's core conflict, not merely postpone it.
4. Use montage intelligently when mythic scale must be conveyed quickly.

## Layout Guardrails
1. Avoid overcrowding pages with equal-sized panels.
2. Use taller panels for vertical scale, rain, falling water, or divine descent.
3. Use wide panels for horizons, oceans, or silence.
4. If a page ends on a reveal, the last panel should be compositionally dominant.

## Final Check Before Prompting
1. Can a reader tell where everyone is?
2. Is the last panel emotionally strongest?
3. Did the page move the chapter forward instead of repeating a previous beat?
